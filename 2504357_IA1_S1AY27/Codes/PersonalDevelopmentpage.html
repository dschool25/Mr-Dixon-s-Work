Reflection on the Chapters

Chapters Three and Five : "The Richest Man in Babylon"

In the book The Richest Man in Babylon by George S. Clason, the chapter entitled "Seven Cures for a Lean Purse" highlights the seven key principles of money-making according to Arkad as follows: saving at least 10 percent of the salary earned, controlling expenditure by discriminating between needs and wants, making investments out of savings using compound interest, keeping one’s investment safe from speculative losses, owning one’s house to reduce living cost, ensuring oneself of income in the future years, and continuous improvement of skill set to boost income. Drawing from these concepts, the story of the son of Arkad, Nomasir, in "The Five Laws of Gold" serves as a vehicle to demonstrate how the wealth increases in greater quantities for those who save ten percent of what they earn, earn their way through working diligently for wise employers, hold fast to prudent investors with expert advice, steer away from strange and unfamiliar ventures, and get rid of those who seek profits from unrealistic ventures or those who con people out of their money. Applying such ancient wisdom to modern-day life, one realizes how essential such behaviors are. The important thing to learn from both the chapters is that it is much better to be disciplined and wise rather than possessing wealth for temporary gains as the key to success lies in discipline and wisdom rather than fortune.
