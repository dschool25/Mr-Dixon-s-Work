    Strong Hardworking and Discipline

  • I work very hards to do great work and keep my grades high. I am reliable and always stick to my tasks, but setting such high standards for myself sometimes make me stressed

    • Self Learning

  • I do my best thinking when i'm working by myself in a quiet space. I like to watch and listen rather than draw attention to myself, so working in big groups isn't always my favourite thing.

    • Cautious

  • I am cautious an prefer to stick with methods I already know work. I don't like taking big risks or failing. I'm hesitant when it comes to trying new ways of learning.

    • Caring but Take things to Heart

  • I care a lot about doing things correctly, so criticism of falling short can hit me hard. However, feeling things so deeply also helps me understand and care when others are having a tough time.

Conclusion

These traits contribute to a disciplined, focused, and efficient academic approach, though they may struggle with flexibility at times, criticism, and the social aspects of academic life.

Quote (Proverbs15:1)

Meaning of the Quote

This proverb emphasizes the power of our tone and words in personal relationships and conflict. The meaning is that responding calmly and thoughtfully during tense moments can defuse anger, lower tension, keep peace between people. On the other hand, meeting hostility with harshness or sharp words only escalates arguments and creates more tension. This verse encourages us to practice patience, self-control, and kindness in our communication so we can build stronger, healthier connections with others.

Essay

"LLMs such as ChatGPT, Claude, and CoPilot must be used as a tool not a crutch"

The Integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) such as ChatGPT, Claude, GitHub, Copilot into higher education marks a major shift in how students learn. The statement "LLMs must be used as a tool, not as a crutch" highlights the line between active learning and passive dependency. For students, understanding this boundary is essential for building real competence and maintaining academic integrity.

In school, tools extend human capability and deepens understanding without replacing the core reasoning process. In contrast, a crutch is relied upon to carry out tasks a student should perform independently. Using AI as a crutch creates a false sense of mastry. Assignments are finished and the code runs but actual critical thinking, problem solving skills, and subject mastery are bypassed.

To maximize academic growth, LLMs should be used strategically without sacrificing intellectual independence.

LLMs excel at simplifying dense prose and complex concepts into thoughtful explanations. After drafting essays or code independently, students can use LLms for feedback and to catch oversights. When stuck on syntax errors or broken logic, LLMs help pinpoint specific errors to keep momentum going.

Copilot or CLaude can explain unfamiliar documentation, suggest refactoring strategies, or generate unit-test boilerplates. LLMs can assist in brainstorming outlines, refining thesis statments, and proofreading self-authored drafts for clarity. AI can function as an interactive tutor, generating practice quizzes to test conceptual understanding.

To ensure AI acts as an amplifier rather than a shortcut, students must adopt deliberate strategies. Prompt the AI to ask guided questions rather than give direct answers. Request real world scenarios to bridge abstract concepts with practical mental models. Submit self-written work and ask for reviews on performance bottlenecks, edge cases, and clarity.

Ultimately, using LLMs as a tool keeps human critical thinking at the center of learning. Higher education aims to build the internal mental muscle and analytical skills necessary for solving complex real world problems. By using AI to brainstorm, clarify and critique rather than replace personal effoert, students become capable, efficient, and self direcred professionals.
